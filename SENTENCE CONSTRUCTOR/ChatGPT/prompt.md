Clues for word order & grammar
Sentence 1: "Bears are at the door"

Sanskrit word order: In classic Sanskrit, we usually go: Subject → Adverb/Location → Verb. So "bears" comes first, "at the door" next, and "are" at the end.
"Bears": Multiple bears = plural. Subject of sentence → prathamā vibhakti / nominative case.
"at the door": Location → saptamī vibhakti / locative case. "Door" + "at" becomes one word with saptamī ending.
"are": Present tense, plural. For "to be", Sanskrit often uses √as in laṭ lakar / present tense. You can also skip the verb entirely in simple statements - Sanskrit does that a lot.
Sentence 2: "Did you leave the garbage out?"

This is a question, but Sanskrit doesn't change word order for yes/no questions. You can add kim at the start, or use intonation.
Word order: Kim → Subject → Object → Adverb → Verb
"you": Subject → prathamā vibhakti. But if it's clear from the verb, you can drop it.
"leave": Past action, completed → laṅ lakar / past tense, or use kta past participle. The root is √tyaj or √muc.
"the garbage": Object of leaving → dvitīyā vibhakti / accusative case.
"out": Means "outside". This becomes an adverb → bahiḥ.
